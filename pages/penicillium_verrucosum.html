Penicillium verrucosum

← Back

If your bread reaks of soil or has green spots, Penicillium verrucosum might be the cause. This mold is in the same family as molds used in cheese, as well as molds that create penicilin. However, P. verrucosum is not suitable for consumption or medicinal purposes. P. verrucosum contains an active compound called Ochratoxin A, which can mutate cells, causing cancer. It has also been suspected to cause Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.

Being a grain saprobe, it can also end up in animal food. This can be concerning because it may end up in pig food, which could leave significant amounts of the toxin in their fat.

Other than Ochratoxin A P. verrucosum also contains citrinin, which can damage the kindeys.
