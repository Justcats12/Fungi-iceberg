This story is probably the most tragic mushroom related story, which has barely been reported on in mainstream media. On 8 September 1918 a class of young boys, aged 7 to 14 years old, stumbled onto a patch of mushrooms while walking to school from church. Hungry and excited with this discovery, they took the mushrooms to their school cook, who made the mistake of assuming the mushrooms were safe to eat based on a taste test. Afterwards she prepared a dish for 331 schoolboys. That night some of the boys started to throw up revealing the true nature of what they had eaten. Over the course of the next week they died one by one in the local hospital. In total, 31 students died of mushroom poisoning, only two of the boys having survived.

The likely culprit is the death cap since it is common all across europe. Moreover, it cannot be identified as deadly with a taste test.

Two years later, in 1920, a memorial was built for them at the local cemetery.



1The official papers at the time claim 34 children had eaten the dish, but later sources state 33.
