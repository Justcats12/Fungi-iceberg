Lepiota locaniensis

← Back

Lepiota locaniensis was first reported in 1935 as a new Chilean fungus. Mycologists kept finding the L. locaniensis untill 1946, having kept note of the locations it would typically grow in. Between 1964 and 1975 attempts were made to find it again, to no success. Since it is a poisonous species, researches tried to find the fungus in medical records of mushroom poisonings, but no record of the mushroom had been found there either.

In 1994 Simonetti and Lazo published a paper announcing L. locaniensis was formally presumed extinct. The reason for it's dissapearance is unknown.
