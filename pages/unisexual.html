Unisexual death caps

The death cap is invasive in many parts of the world, such as America and Australia. Because of this rapid spread, researchers in California decided to take a closer look at this deadly mushroom.

To simplify the sexual reproduction of Fungi similar to the death cap, you need two desitinct induviduals to create a mushroom. Two spores that reach out to each other in the form of mycelium, then fuse into one ready to sexually reproduce. This is what fungi like the death cap and fly agaric need to do for creating offspring.

What researchers discovered is that this romantic two-spore story doesn't have to be true, at least in Amanita phalloides (death cap). This fungus is capable of having a singular spore reach out to itself in what I can only assume is a mycelium loop. After which it is perfectly capable of reproducting with itself, basically cloning itself via fruiting body. This behaviour has only been witnessed in Californian invasive specimen, and it's unclear weither european death caps do this too. They are the same species so one can only assume.

So in short, the death cap can spread so rapidly across new areas because it can create spores without needing a partner.
