Angel wings

← Back

Oyster mushrooms are some of the safest, if not the safest, mushrooms for foraging. In most places they have only one lookalike: Preurocybellae, commonly known as angel wings.

For the longest time this mushroom was considered edible, some guides even stated it was a choice edible. This ended in September 2004, when 17 people in Japan died from eating angel wings. In America people had been eating them for decades and now suddenly they become fatal. The victims were all Japanese and most had liver or kidney problems, yet they died from fatal brain damage.

The suspected poison is pleurocybellaziridin, all though it is uncertain how this compound works exactly. Why have there been no deaths reported from the exact same species in America? Whatever mystery this fungus is hiding, it wouldn't be the first fungus to switch from 'Edible' to 'Deadly' in the guidebooks. In the 1980s René Flammer discovered that the widely consumed brown roll-rim contained a compound that caused autoimmune reactions. This would take so long to kill the patient that for decades people didn't suspect the mushroom as the cause.
