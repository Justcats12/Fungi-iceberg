The pezica

← Back

Pliny the elder was a Roman writer dedicating his life to writing down all knowledge at the time. While he writes a bunch of stuff that didn't turn out to be true, he also writes a lot of surprisingly correct facts. For example, about fungi he can correctly say that specimens growing close to metals become toxic. But a few sentences later he says the same about mushrooms growing near the hole of a snake. These are not the only 'facts' he writes about fungi. In fact he dedicates a very short chapter to a mushroom the greeks called "pezica".

The only thing he describes the pezica as is a mushroom that grows without a stalk. It's importance to the greeks could suggest that it's an edible species, such as a puffball or a jelly ear. Some even think that it's a morel. Whatever the species, with this vague of a description we'll likely never know unless the name pops up in an earlier greek source.

Funnily enough, researchers took it upon themselves to create the pezica themselves. In 2020 named a new species of bovista after it. Bovista pezica is an Argentinian species, so it can't be the one described by the Roman researcher.
