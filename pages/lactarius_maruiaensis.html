Lactarius maruiaensis

← Back

The year is 1968 and you're a mycologist from New Zealand. You stumble across a yellow milk cap that looks strange to you. It doesn't look like the safron milk caps that you're used to. You decide to take a sample. To your surprise it's an undescribed new species! You quickly save the sample and send it to a research storage center. It receives the name "Lactarius maruiaensis".

50 years later and nobody has ever seen L. maruiaensis fruit again. It is now considered extinct. McNabb being the first and last person to ever make a record of this milk cap. We'll never know enough about it to know why it went extinct, in fact, it might still be alive, hiding deep in the New Zealand woods.
